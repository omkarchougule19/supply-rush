## 1. Game Setup & Phases
* **Starting Cash:** $3,000,000
* **Total Quarters:** 16 (default)
* **Quarter 0 (Setup Phase):** 
  - Time for initial planning and placing warehouses.
  - Zero active demand is simulated and no delivery animations play.
  - **Golden Circles (Revealed Zones):** Dash outline markers indicating possible demand zones are visible ONLY in Quarter 0 to assist with initial placement. They do not appear during active play quarters (Q1+).

## 2. Warehouses
Warehouses have different costs, capacities, build times, overhead costs, and sell-back values:

| Type | Purchase Cost | Capacity (Orders/Qtr) | Build Time | Overhead Cost/Qtr | Sell-Back Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Small** | $250,000 | 500 | 1 Quarter | $20,000 | $125,000 |
| **Medium** | $500,000 | 1,200 | 2 Quarters | $30,000 | $250,000 |
| **Large** | $800,000 | 2,500 | 4 Quarters | $40,000 | $400,000 |

* **Build Time Rules:** A warehouse built at quarter `B` becomes active and usable at the start of quarter `B + build_time`.
* **Fleet Capacity Cap:** A warehouse's total fleet capacity (sum of all vehicles parked at it) cannot exceed the warehouse's own capacity rating.
* **Overhead Cost:** Every **active** warehouse (built and not sold — warehouses still under construction are exempt) charges its fixed overhead cost every quarter regardless of order volume. This is folded into `operating_cost` and also tracked separately (`overhead_cost`) so it appears as its own line item in the quarterly results and as its own segment in the Game Over "Spending Breakdown" chart. It exists to create a real fixed-vs-variable cost trade-off — more facilities mean wider coverage but a higher fixed bill every quarter, discouraging students from over-building carelessly.

## 3. Fleet Vehicles
Vehicles handle deliveries from warehouses. Drones are the only vehicles that can deliver urgent orders.

| Type | Purchase Cost | Capacity (Orders/Qtr) | Operating Cost/Qtr | Sell-Back Value | Serves Urgent | Serves Non-Urgent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Truck** | $20,000 | 200 | $800 | $12,000 | No | Yes |
| **Drone** | $9,000 | 60 | $800 | $5,400 | Yes | Yes |

* **Shared/Flexible Drone Capacity:** Drones serve both urgent and non-urgent orders. Their capacity is shared, not double-counted (e.g. a single drone can deliver 40 urgent and 20 non-urgent orders in a quarter, but not 60 of each).
* **Same-Quarter Relocations vs. Depreciation**: Normally, if a player buys and sells a vehicle of the same type within the same quarter, the transaction is netted to $0 (representing a free relocation of assets between warehouses). If the instructor turns ON the **"Allowing moving trucks & drones"** toggle in the scenario configurations, same-quarter netting is disabled, and players will face the full depreciation penalty (paying full purchase cost and receiving only sell-back credit) for every purchase/sale transaction.

## 4. Revenues & Third-Party Logistics (3PL) Outsourcing
* **Fulfillment Revenues:**
  - **Urgent Order:** $55 per order delivered
  - **Non-Urgent Order:** $25 per order delivered
* **Outsourcing Option (3PL):**
  - Allowed in all modes including the default normal game.
  - Players can toggle individual demand zones to be outsourced.
  - **Outsource Costs:** $75 per urgent order, $40 per non-urgent order (or custom rates set in scenario configs).
  - **Outsource Deliveries:** Outsourced orders are 100% fulfilled instantly, completely bypassing warehouse capacity limits and fleet operating expenses.
  - **Persistence & Remaining Demand**: Outsourced zones remain outsourced across quarters until toggled off. Since they are 100% fulfilled instantly, they always end a quarter run with exactly `0` remaining demand.

## 5. Service & Delivery Routing
* **Service Radius:** A warehouse can only serve demand zones located within its service radius (default: 30 units distance, calculated using Euclidean formula).
* **Fulfillment Allocation:** 
  1. Zones are assigned to the nearest active warehouse within the service radius.
  2. Urgent demand is fulfilled first by drone capacity.
  3. Non-urgent demand is fulfilled by remaining drone capacity and truck capacity.
  4. Fulfillments are capped by the warehouse's overall throughput capacity and its parked vehicles' capacities.

## 6. Demand Generation & Evolution
* **Zone Selection:** Each play randomly selects 60% of the scenario's master demand-zone list (the default master list has 70 zones, so a play typically works with 42) — a different random 60% for every play, fixed for that play's whole game once chosen.
* **Active Zone Scaling:** The number of active demand zones scales linearly from 50% of eligible zones in Quarter 1 to 100% by the second-to-last quarter.
* **Eligible Pools:** 50% of the game's selected zones are revealed at Q0. The other 50% unlock progressively during gameplay (starting at Q6).
* **Demand Evolution (Active Zones):**
  - **Growth (85% probability):** Demand grows by 5% to 20% compared to the previous quarter.
  - **Drop (15% probability):** Demand drops by 50% to 80% compared to the previous quarter.
  - Clamped between a minimum of 10 and a configured maximum (default: 800) orders.

## 7. Instructor Pacing Controls: Quarter Gating
* **Purpose:** Prevent individual groups from racing through quarters ahead of the rest of the class. The instructor can require every group to advance in lockstep.
* **Mechanic:** Each `Scenario` has an `unlocked_quarter` value. `0` (the default, for every existing and newly created scenario) means gating is **off** — plays advance freely, exactly as before this feature existed. `N >= 1` means any play under that scenario code may run/start quarters up to and including `N`, but not beyond.
* **Shared scope:** The gate lives on the **scenario** (the class code), not on an individual play. Every group that joined with the same code shares one gate — one instructor action re-paces the whole class at once.
* **Instructor controls (dashboard):** Each scenario row shows its gating status plus buttons: "Enable Quarter Gating" (sets `unlocked_quarter` to 1), "Unlock Next Quarter" (increments it by 1), and "Disable Gating" (resets to 0, restoring free play).
* **Student experience:** While the next quarter isn't yet unlocked, the "Run Quarter" / "Start Game" button is simply disabled and reads "🔒 Waiting for Instructor" — no error is surfaced, the click never reaches the backend. The client polls the scenario's gate status every 7 seconds so the button re-enables shortly after the instructor unlocks it. Gating never applies to offline demo mode.
* **Enforcement:** `POST /sessions/{play_id}/advance` independently rejects (`403`) any attempt to run a quarter beyond `unlocked_quarter`, as a safety net for stale client state (e.g. a second open tab) — this should not normally be visible to a student following the disabled-button UI.

## 8. Access Control & Student Verification
Two independent, unrelated gates protect the app. Both are additive — neither changes any game mechanic documented above.

* **Instructor lock (HTTP Basic Auth):** The `/instructor` page and every instructor-tagged API endpoint (`POST/GET/PUT/DELETE /scenarios*`, `GET /plays`, `GET /sessions/{id}/warehouses`) require a username/password from `INSTRUCTOR_USERNAMES`/`INSTRUCTOR_PASSWORDS` env vars, checked via the browser's native Basic Auth popup. No database table, no "account" — matches a single instructor (or a small named team) managing scenarios. `GET /scenarios/{code}` (looking up ONE known code) is deliberately left public — the quarter-gating status poll (Section 7) depends on it, and knowing a specific code already functions like a shared secret; only *listing every code* is instructor-only.
* **Student verification (passwordless email codes):** Controlled by `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION` (default `false` — the whole feature is inert until an instructor turns it on for a deployment). When on:
  - A student must verify a school email (restricted to `ALLOWED_STUDENT_EMAIL_DOMAIN`, default `illinois.edu`) via a 6-digit code sent by email (Resend) before starting or joining any play. No password is ever created.
  - Verifying sets a signed session cookie (`SESSION_SECRET`-signed, 7-day expiry) so the student doesn't need to re-verify on every visit.
* **Group membership, not single ownership:** This game is played by groups, and — critically — there is no existing "share a link with your teammate" mechanism in the app (checked: `play_id` was never placed in the URL, localStorage, or anywhere retrievable by a teammate). So group-joining had to be built from scratch rather than layered onto something that already worked. The mechanism: a student entering a scenario code also enters a free-text **Group Name**; every student who submits the same `(scenario code, group name)` pair (case-insensitive) lands in the SAME shared `Play` — looked up fresh on every submission, which doubles as the reconnect mechanism (no URL/localStorage tracking needed at all). Each verified email that joins becomes a row in `PlayMember`; a play action is rejected with `403` unless the requester's session email is a member of that specific play. Membership is capped at `MAX_GROUP_SIZE` (default 60) per play. Normal mode (no scenario code) has no group concept — every play there is solo, matching its existing "casual play" design intent.
* **Fails safe to "off":** Every server-side check (`require_student_email`, `_ensure_member`, `_require_member`) is a no-op whenever `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION` is `false`, using a stable placeholder email internally — so local development, and any deployment that never sets the flag, behaves exactly as it did before this feature existed. Group-Name joining itself still works even with verification off (a genuinely useful bonus — students can share a play without needing the email gate), it's only the identity-proof and the membership cap that switch on with the flag.

## 9. Random Disruption Events
* **Purpose:** Real supply chains face uncertainty — natural disasters, weather, fuel prices — that a static optimization exercise doesn't teach. Random disruption events introduce quarter-scoped shocks students must plan around.
* **Off by default:** Controlled by `Scenario.disruption_config` (`{"enabled": bool, "events": {key: {enabled, probability, severity_min, severity_max}}}`). `enabled: false` (the default for every scenario) means zero behavior change from before this feature existed.
* **Three built-in event types** (`game_logic.DISRUPTION_EVENT_TYPES`):
  | Key | Effect | Scope |
  |---|---|---|
  | `earthquake_warehouse` | Cuts effective vehicle capacity at ONE warehouse **map slot** by a random severity | Localized — only a play with an active warehouse at the targeted slot is affected |
  | `harsh_winter_drone` | Cuts every drone's effective capacity fleet-wide | Every play under the scenario, if they own any drones |
  | `fuel_hike_truck` | Raises every truck's effective operating cost fleet-wide | Every play under the scenario, if they own any trucks |
* **Shared per scenario, not per play:** Each quarter, every event type is rolled independently (`roll_disruption_events`), seeded by `scenario_id * 1000 + quarter` — identical for every group sharing a scenario code, so the *event* (which slot, what severity, which quarter) is the same for everyone. The *impact* still differs per group: a group with no warehouse at the earthquake's target slot feels nothing from it, and a group with a warehouse there does. Multiple event types can fire the same quarter (independent rolls, not mutually exclusive).
* **Forecast, not surprise:** The upcoming quarter's events are exposed via the same `GET /sessions/{id}/demand` preview call already used for the demand forecast, so students see "⚠️ Earthquake near slot s7 — warehouse capacity cut 30% this quarter" *before* clicking Run Quarter/Start Game — a decision they can react to (delay a purchase there, lean on a different location), not just an after-the-fact number.
* **Mechanism:** Fired events are turned into per-play multipliers (`compute_disruption_modifiers`) — `warehouse_capacity_multipliers` (keyed by this play's own `PlacedWarehouse.id`), `vehicle_capacity_multipliers` and `vehicle_cost_multipliers` (keyed by vehicle type). `simulate_quarter()` applies them when computing each vehicle's effective capacity/operating cost for that quarter only — nothing persists into the following quarter, and a disabled scenario's multipliers are all `1.0` (no-op).
* **Persistence:** Which events fired (and actually touched a given play) is written to `QuarterResult.disruption_events` and the `Play.quarterly_results` JSON mirror at `/advance` time, so a later instructor edit to `disruption_config` never rewrites what already happened to a group.
* **Instructor controls:** A master "Enable Random Disruption Events" toggle plus per-event enabled/probability/severity-range tuning in the scenario editor.

### Feature: Fleet Capacity vs Demand Over Time Chart (Game Over screen)
* **Idea:** None of the existing Game Over charts showed absolute capacity — only relative utilization % (Fleet Utilization, Avg Utilization by Warehouse Category) or fulfillment counts (Orders Delivered, % Demand Satisfied). The actual binding constraint on fulfillment in `simulate_quarter()` is **fleet capacity** (`total_vehicle_capacity` — the sum of active vehicles' capacity), not the warehouse's own capacity rating: `utilization_rate = orders_fulfilled / total_vehicle_capacity` already proves this, and warehouse capacity is only used to cap how many vehicles can be stationed there, not to cap fulfillment directly. So the clearest way to show "capacity over time" is a chart plotting **Fleet Capacity** (the ceiling) against **Total Demand** (orders_total, including outsourced orders) — wherever the demand line pokes above the filled capacity area, that quarter was capacity-constrained.

### Feature: Access Control — Instructor Basic Auth + Passwordless Student Email Verification
* **Idea:** The instructor asked for two things: (1) lock the instructor dashboard so only they can define scenarios, and (2) some way to know students are real, verified members of the target institution, without making students create passwords. See Section 8 above for the full mechanic description; this entry records the design reasoning and how it was built.
* **Design decisions made while planning (before writing code):**
  1. **Instructor auth reused an existing, unimplemented blueprint.** `future_plans.md` already specified HTTP Basic Auth with env-var credentials for exactly this purpose — no reason to design something new for a single admin.
  2. **Student identity check chosen over full "accounts."** Modeled after a colleague's prior implementation elsewhere: email a one-time code, no password ever created or stored, restricted to a configurable school domain. Deliberately NOT a persistent account system — nothing survives beyond a signed session cookie and a `PlayMember` row.
  3. **The critical scoping question was resolved by checking the code, not assuming:** the instructor wanted each group member to be able to join independently from their own device. Checking `game.html` confirmed there is **no existing way for a student to share a play with a teammate at all** — `play_id` was never placed in the URL, hash, or localStorage; every browser tab creates a brand-new play. So "independent join" couldn't be layered onto something that worked — group membership had to be designed from scratch. The chosen mechanism (a free-text **Group Name** entered alongside the scenario code, looked up case-insensitively per scenario to find-or-create the shared play) was picked specifically because it requires **no new client-side state at all** — no URL param, no localStorage, no share links — every group member just submits the same two strings, which also happens to double as the reconnect mechanism for free.
  4. **Every check fails open when the feature is off.** `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION` defaults to `false`; every server-side gate (`require_student_email`, `_ensure_member`, `_require_member`) becomes a no-op in that state, so this entire feature is inert — including for local development and any deployment that never sets the flag — until an instructor deliberately turns it on.
* **Working Steps:**
  1. **Database (`models.py`, `main.py`):**
     - Added `Play.group_name` (nullable, indexed) plus a startup migration.
     - Added two new tables: `PlayMember` (`play_id_fk`, `email`, `joined_at`, unique on the pair) — the group's verified roster — and `VerificationCode` (`email`, hashed code, `expires_at`, `attempts_remaining`, `consumed`) — short-lived, single-use login codes. Both are brand-new tables, so no `ALTER TABLE` migration was needed (`Base.metadata.create_all` handles new tables automatically).
     - `Play.delete_scenario()` in `routes.py` was updated to also bulk-delete `PlayMember` rows for a scenario's plays before deleting the plays themselves, to avoid orphaned rows now that a real FK exists.
  2. **New module (`auth.py`):** Self-contained module holding both gates:
     - `authenticate_instructor()` — the `future_plans.md` Basic Auth blueprint, essentially verbatim.
     - `require_student_email()` — a FastAPI dependency returning the session's verified email, or a stable placeholder (`"unverified@local"`) when verification is off, or a `401` when verification is on and no valid cookie is present.
     - Session cookie signing via `itsdangerous.URLSafeTimedSerializer` (`SESSION_SECRET`-keyed, 7-day expiry, HttpOnly + Secure + SameSite=Lax).
     - Code generation/hashing/verification (`create_verification_code`, `verify_code`) — codes are stored as SHA-256 hashes salted with the email, never in plaintext; rate-limited to one request per email per 60 seconds; capped at 5 wrong-guess attempts before a code is dead.
     - `send_verification_email()` — POSTs to the Resend API; falls back to logging the code to the server console when `RESEND_API_KEY` is unset, so the verification flow itself can be exercised locally without a real email provider.
  3. **API (`schemas.py`, `routes.py`):**
     - New schemas `EmailCodeRequest`/`EmailCodeVerify`; `PlayCreate` gained an optional `group_name` field.
     - New endpoints: `POST /auth/request-code`, `POST /auth/verify-code`, `GET /auth/me` (lets the frontend check verification status without triggering a 401), `POST /auth/logout`.
     - Instructor-gated (`dependencies=[Depends(authenticate_instructor)]`): `POST/PUT/DELETE /scenarios*`, `GET /scenarios` (list), `GET /plays`. Deliberately left public: `GET /scenarios/{code}` (single lookup) — the quarter-gating status poll added in Section 7 depends on this endpoint staying reachable without credentials, and knowing one specific code already functions like a shared secret; only *enumerating every code* needed locking down.
     - Re-tagged `GET /sessions/{play_id}/warehouses` from `student` to `instructor` — checking `instructor_dashboard.html` showed this endpoint is only ever called by the play-details modal, never by the student game, so it was mistagged from the start; it's now Basic-Auth-gated instead of student-membership-gated (an instructor isn't a "member" of any student's play).
     - `start_play()` (`POST /sessions`) now does find-or-create: if a non-empty `group_name` is given, it looks for an existing incomplete play under the same scenario with a case-insensitive-matching group name and joins it (adding the caller as a `PlayMember`, capped at `MAX_GROUP_SIZE`) instead of always creating a new one. `start_normal_play()` stays solo-only (no group concept, matching its existing "casual play" design intent) but still records the first/only member when verification is on.
     - Every other play-scoped student endpoint (`GET /sessions/{id}`, `/outsource`, `/warehouses`, `/vehicles`, `/warehouses/sell`, `/vehicles/sell`, `/demand`, `/advance`, `/history`) now takes `email: str = Depends(require_student_email)` and calls `_require_member()` before doing anything — rejecting with `403` if the caller isn't a member of that specific play. Both `_ensure_member` (adds + caps) and `_require_member` (checks only) are no-ops when verification is off.
     - `list_all_plays()` (`GET /plays`) now also returns `group_name` and `member_emails` per play, for instructor visibility.
  4. **Frontend — instructor (`instructor_dashboard.html`):** No auth-specific code needed — HTTP Basic Auth credentials, once entered via the browser's native popup (triggered by `main.py`'s now-gated `/instructor` page route), are automatically attached by the browser to same-origin `fetch()` calls for the rest of the session. Only change: the plays table's "Student" column now shows the group name plus a verified-member count (with emails in a hover tooltip) when a play has a `group_name`, falling back to the old student-name display otherwise.
  5. **Frontend — student (`game.html`):**
     - New `#verify-screen` (email input → code input → verify), hidden by default alongside `#entry-screen` (previously always visible on load); a new `checkAuthAndShowEntry()` call on page `load` hits `GET /auth/me` and decides which screen to show — failing open to the entry screen if the backend is unreachable, so offline demo mode stays reachable even if verification is on and the server is down.
     - Added a **Group Name** input to the existing "enter a class code" section of the entry screen; `startWithCode()` now sends it as part of the `POST /sessions` body.
     - **Bug caught while wiring this in:** both `startNormalMode()` and `startWithCode()` originally treated *any* fetch failure — including a `401` (session expired/never verified) — as "backend unreachable," which for `startNormalMode()` meant silently falling back to unauthenticated offline demo mode. That would have completely defeated the point of requiring verification (a student whose 7-day cookie expired mid-course would get bounced into an unscored, untracked demo with no explanation). Fixed by checking `r.status === 401` explicitly and re-showing the verify screen in that case, only falling back to demo mode on a genuine network-level failure.
  6. **Config (`requirements.txt`, `.env.example`):** Added `itsdangerous` and `requests` as dependencies. Created `.env.example` (didn't exist before) documenting every env var this feature introduced — `INSTRUCTOR_USERNAMES`/`INSTRUCTOR_PASSWORDS`, `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION`, `ALLOWED_STUDENT_EMAIL_DOMAIN`, `SESSION_SECRET`, `COOKIE_SECURE`, `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM`, `MAX_GROUP_SIZE` — alongside the existing `DATABASE_URL`/`ALLOWED_ORIGINS`.
  7. **`future_plans.md`** was annotated as implemented (not deleted) pointing back to this entry, since it was the source blueprint for the instructor-auth half of this feature.
  8. **Verification:** Ran two isolated API sessions (separate ports + scratch DBs from the live dev database):
     - **With `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION=true`, `MAX_GROUP_SIZE=2`:** `/instructor` and `/api/scenarios*` correctly `401` without Basic Auth credentials and `200` with them; `/api/scenarios/{code}` (single lookup) stayed public as designed. A non-`@test.edu` email was rejected with `400`; a valid one received a logged 6-digit code (no `RESEND_API_KEY` set, confirming the dev fallback works); a wrong code was rejected, the right code succeeded and was correctly single-use (rejected on replay). `POST /sessions` with no session cookie at all returned `401`. Two different verified students (`alice@test.edu`, `bob@test.edu`) submitting the same `(scenario_code, group_name)` pair — with different casing (`TeamA` vs `teama`) — landed in the exact same `play_id`, and both could act on it. A third student (`carol@test.edu`) was correctly rejected joining that group (`400`, "group is full") once `MAX_GROUP_SIZE=2` was reached, and was correctly rejected (`403`) attempting to act on the play directly without being a member. The instructor's `GET /plays` correctly showed `group_name: "TeamA"` and `member_emails: ["alice@test.edu", "bob@test.edu"]`. The re-tagged `GET /sessions/{id}/warehouses` correctly required instructor Basic Auth instead of student membership.

### Feature: Random Disruption Events
* **Idea:** The instructor wanted real-world supply-chain uncertainty in the game — a localized event (e.g. an earthquake damaging one warehouse), a fleet-wide capacity hit (e.g. harsh winter grounding drones), and a fleet-wide cost shock (e.g. a fuel price hike raising truck operating costs) — so a quarter can go sideways even with a sound plan. See Section 9 above for the full mechanic description.
* **Design decisions made while planning:**
  1. **Forecast, not surprise** (user's explicit choice): shown on the existing pre-quarter demand-preview call, not sprung only in results, so it's a strategic decision point, not just noise.
  2. **Shared across the scenario, not per-play** (user's explicit choice, for the same fairness reason as the demand-seed discussion): seeded off `scenario_id`, not `play.id`. A localized event needed a target that's meaningful across plays with different warehouse layouts, so `earthquake_warehouse` targets a **map slot ID** (scenario-wide, fixed by the instructor) rather than a specific play's warehouse row — a play is only affected if it happens to have a warehouse at that slot, which is a deliberate feature (rewards diversified placement), not an inconsistency.
  3. **Fully tunable per event type, plus one master switch** (user's explicit choice): every new gate in this codebase fails open until an instructor turns it on (same principle as `REQUIRE_STUDENT_VERIFICATION`) — `disruption_config.enabled` defaults to `false`.
* **Working Steps:**
  1. **Game Logic (`game_logic.py`):** Added `DISRUPTION_EVENT_TYPES` (registry: kind, vehicle_type, label, message template), `DEFAULT_DISRUPTION_CONFIG`, `roll_disruption_events()` (deterministic per `scenario_id`+quarter, fixed sorted-key iteration order so the RNG draw sequence is stable), `compute_disruption_modifiers()` (scenario-level fired events → this play's concrete multipliers by matching `target_slot_id` against the play's own warehouses), and `events_affecting_play()` (filters the shared event list down to what's relevant for a specific play's forecast/results display). `simulate_quarter()` gained an optional `disruption_modifiers` param applied to each vehicle's effective capacity/cost inside the existing per-warehouse loop — refactored `operating_cost`/`drone_cost`/`truck_cost` to sum from that same per-vehicle effective-cost data instead of re-parsing `wh.vehicles` JSON a second time via the now-removed `get_vehicle_operating_cost()` helper (dead after the refactor, deleted).
  2. **Database (`models.py`, `main.py`):** Added `Scenario.disruption_config` (Text, JSON) and `QuarterResult.disruption_events` (Text, JSON) plus startup migrations for both.
  3. **API (`schemas.py`, `routes.py`):** Added `DisruptionEventConfig`/`DisruptionConfig` Pydantic models (with `severity_min <= severity_max` and `0 <= probability <= 1` validators) and a `disruption_config` field on `ScenarioCreate`/`ScenarioUpdate`/`ScenarioOut`. `GET /sessions/{id}/demand` and `POST /sessions/{id}/advance` both roll the same scenario-seeded events for the relevant quarter, then narrow to this play's actual impact via `events_affecting_play`; `/advance` also persists the fired events into `QuarterResult` and the `quarterly_results` JSON mirror.
  4. **Frontend (`game.html`):** Added a "⚠️ Disruption Forecast" card in the left sidebar (populated from the `/demand` preview response) and a matching results-sidebar callout (from the `/advance` response), reusing the existing `r-activated-callout` visual pattern. Offline demo mode (`localSim()`) deliberately does not roll events — no real scenario/DB row to seed off, and it's already a stripped-down fallback.
  5. **Frontend (`instructor_dashboard.html`):** Added a "🌪️ Random Disruption Events" scenario-editor section — one master toggle plus a 3-row table (event enabled / probability / severity min / severity max) for the three built-in types, wired into the existing `collectForm()`/`resetForm()` functions.
  6. **Verification:** Ran an isolated API session (separate port + scratch DB): forced all three event probabilities to 0.95+, created two plays under the same scenario with warehouses at different map slots, and confirmed the same quarter on both plays reported the identical `earthquake_warehouse` target slot and severity (shared-scenario roll working), only the play with a warehouse at that slot saw a capacity hit in its actual result numbers (fleet capacity correctly compounded both the earthquake's warehouse-level multiplier and harsh winter's fleet-wide multiplier — e.g. a 60-capacity drone under both an active 40% winter cut and a 30% earthquake cut at its warehouse correctly computed to `round(60*0.6*0.7)=25`), the fleet-wide events affected both plays identically regardless of slot, and a scenario with `disruption_config.enabled=false` (the default) produced results identical to before this feature existed. Followed up with a full browser click-through confirming both the pre-quarter forecast card and the post-quarter results callout render correctly, and that the default (no-code) Normal Mode scenario shows no forecast (disabled by default there too).
